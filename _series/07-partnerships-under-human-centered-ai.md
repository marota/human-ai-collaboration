The [previous post]({{ '/series/' | relative_url }}) established a core rule for high-stakes environments: AI should partner, not replace. But that raises a critical question — how much control should the human actually keep?

Ben Shneiderman offers a refreshing Human-Centered AI framework for this. His key insight is to stop treating automation as a single slider moving from "manual" to "autonomous." Instead, he splits it into two distinct dimensions: the level of machine automation, and the degree of human control. These are not opposites. The goal is the rare top-right corner — high automation *and* high human control — rather than a forced trade-off between the two.

My favourite analogy from his work is the control room. Think of a modern flight deck or a power-grid control centre: large automation runs in the background, humans actively manage and see the whole picture, and operators can anticipate and step in instantly before anything runs wild. The automation serves the operators; it doesn't sideline them.

That's the bar for responsible AI in high-stakes work: powerful underneath, managed on top, with a human who can always read the situation and take the controls.
