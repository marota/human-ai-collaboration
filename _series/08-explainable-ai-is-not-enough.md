Most AI decision tools answer the wrong question. They tell you *what* to do. They should help you think about *why*.

You might think explainable AI is the answer. Yet, as Miller argues in *Explainable AI is Dead, Long Live Explainable AI!*, a far more effective approach is hypothesis-driven evaluative AI. It doesn't hand you a verdict — it helps you form a hypothesis, then surfaces the evidence for and against it. The expert stays the one reasoning; the AI makes that reasoning faster and more rigorous.

Toni Wäfler's Supportive AI framework, developed within the AI4REALNET project, draws the line even more cleanly:

- **Supportive AI** strengthens the human's judgment.
- **Substitutive AI** quietly replaces it.

Same accuracy on a one-time benchmark — opposite effect on the expert over time.

The through-line of this series: a good tool leaves the human sharper, not more dependent.
