A new controlled study from Carnegie Mellon, Oxford, MIT, and UCLA measured this directly, and the findings are a wake-up call: *AI Assistance Reduces Persistence and Hurts Independent Performance.*

Here is what the researchers observed. After just ~10 minutes of AI-assisted problem-solving, participants who then lost access to the AI performed worse and gave up more often than those who had never used it at all. This held true across both arithmetic and reading comprehension.

The crucial, actionable detail: only those who asked the AI to solve the problem suffered, because they had come to depend on an always-available instant helper. Those who used it solely for hints did not.

Persistence — the willingness to wrestle with a hard problem — is one of the strongest predictors of skill acquisition. It's the foundation all future expertise is built on. And apparently, it can erode in just ten minutes.

This reminds me of my own early struggles, trying to develop experimental algorithms within a monolithic C++ simulation platform before Python took over in the industry. It was painful, but that friction forced me to deeply reflect on the usefulness of abstractions — a foundational principle I still rely on, and that I might not have developed otherwise.
