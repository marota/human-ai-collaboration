This post closes the cycle.

The [first four posts]({{ '/series/' | relative_url }}) raised the doubts: cognitive surrender, eroded skill, AI that tells us what we want to hear. The five that followed answered with principles — don't automate carelessly (Bainbridge), design for partnership (Mackay), aim for high automation *and* high control (Shneiderman), support the expert's hypotheses (Wäfler), open the joint decision instead of treating it as a black box (Rasmussen, Lundberg).

One thread runs through all of them: good AI leaves the human sharper and in command, not more dependent.

But the question raised in the [fourth post]({{ '/series/' | relative_url }}) is still open — ultimately, who decides? Principles alone won't answer it. They need a place to be designed, stress-tested and certified before they reach a control room.

The [explainer video]({{ '/video/' | relative_url }}) is my personal, cross-industry answer — and it was itself built through a real human-AI collaboration. It sketches two pillars:

- **A CERN-like research centre** for intelligence augmentation and human-AI science.
- **A Eurocontrol-like simulation infrastructure**, where human-AI collaboration is designed, tested and certified before it is deployed in critical systems.

AI is no longer a bicycle for the mind. It is becoming a cognitive rocket. Rockets need a Mission Control.

That is where the work goes next: where this is already running on real infrastructure, how to make it tangible, who orchestrates humans and agents, and the [map of the European ecosystem]({{ '/map/' | relative_url }}) anyone can contribute to.
