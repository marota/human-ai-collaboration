## What it covers

AI is moving from tools into decision systems shaping critical infrastructure:
transport, energy, healthcare, telecoms, industry, defence. The risk is not that
these systems fail loudly, but that they produce *assisted humans* — operators who
retain formal authority while losing the practised judgement that authority
depends on.

The video argues that addressing this needs shared capability rather than isolated
projects, and sketches two complementary pillars:

- **A multidisciplinary research organisation** dedicated to intelligence
  augmentation and human–AI science, on the model of what CERN did for
  experimental physics.
- **A large European AI facility** for realistic, large-scale simulation — where
  human–AI collaboration can be designed, stress-tested and evaluated before it
  reaches an operational control room, in the spirit of Eurocontrol's validation
  environments.

The claim underneath: Europe's advantage will not come from larger models, but
from better-evidenced collaboration between humans and machines — and the AI Act
creates the conditions to make that evidence a requirement rather than an option.
